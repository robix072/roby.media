Plenty of local business owners assume TikTok is for teenagers and big brands. In practice, it has also become a search engine: people type "best brunch in Cluj", "dentist Iași" or "bathroom renovation Bucharest" straight into the app. If your business shows up there with useful, credible videos, some of those people will call you.

This guide walks you through using TikTok to bring in customers from your own city or area, without becoming a full-time content creator.

## Step 1: set up the account properly

Before your first video, the account needs to be ready to turn viewers into customers.

- **Business account.** Switch to a business account in settings. You get analytics, access to the commercial music library and contact options.
- **Name and username.** Use your business name and, where it helps, your city. For example, your company name followed by "Timisoara". It helps with search and tells visitors straight away where you operate.
- **A clear bio.** In a few words: what you do, for whom and where. Include the city or neighbourhood. Add a simple call to action such as "Book via WhatsApp".
- **Link.** Point to your website, a booking page or directly to WhatsApp. One clear link works better than a long list.
- **Contact details.** Add your phone number and email where the platform allows, so people can reach you without searching.

## Step 2: pick 3 or 4 content pillars

The hardest part isn't filming, it's knowing what to post every week. The fix is to work with a few fixed themes, often called content pillars. For a local business, these almost always work:

1. **Behind the scenes.** How an order is prepared, what a day on site looks like, how the clinic is sanitised. People trust what they can see.
2. **Before and after.** One of the strongest formats for any service: a haircut, a renovation, a treatment, a detailed car.
3. **Answers to real questions.** Every question you get on the phone is a video. "How long does an implant take?", "Which tiles work in a bathroom?", "Do you have a kids' menu?"
4. **Your people.** Introduce your staff, what they do and why they like it. Local customers want to know who they will be dealing with.

On top of these, you can occasionally post offers, events or customer reviews, with their permission.

## Step 3: the first two seconds decide everything

On TikTok, people decide almost instantly whether to stay or scroll. The opening, the hook, has to tell them right away why the video is worth watching. A few structures that work for local businesses:

- "If you live in Brasov and your back hurts, watch this."
- "3 mistakes people make when renovating a bathroom."
- "This is what this flat looked like two weeks ago."
- "The question we get asked most at the clinic."
- "Here's what 50 lei gets you for lunch at our place."

Pair the spoken hook with on-screen text and a first frame that already shows the result or the problem. Skip long intros with a logo and "Hello, we are company X".

## Step 4: formats that work for local businesses

You don't need complicated productions. A handful of simple formats, repeated consistently, deliver the best results:

- **Talking to camera.** The owner or a specialist answers one question. Simple, credible, easy to film on a phone.
- **Sped-up process.** A few hours of work compressed into 20 seconds.
- **Walkthrough.** What a customer sees from the car park to the table or the chair.
- **Reply to a comment.** TikTok lets you answer a comment with a video. It is a great way to turn real questions into content.
- **Series.** "Day 1 of renovating an old house" gives people a reason to come back for the next episode.

For a more polished look, many businesses mix everyday phone footage with one professional shoot per month, which produces a batch of high-quality clips.

## Step 5: posting frequency

Consistency beats perfection. For a local business, a realistic pace is 3 to 5 videos a week. If you can't sustain that, start with 2 a week, but stick to it. An account that posts steadily for three months has far better odds than one that posts daily for two weeks and then goes quiet.

Plan filming ahead: one afternoon a week can be enough to cover the following week's content.

## Step 6: put your city in the text, for search

Because people search TikTok the way they search Google, words matter. Write in your caption, and say in the video:

- the name of your city and, where relevant, the neighbourhood;
- the exact service, in the words customers use ("tile fitting", not just "finishes");
- a specific question or problem.

For example: "Full bathroom renovation in Iasi, from demolition to fitting. How long it took and what we used." Add a few relevant hashtags with the city and your field, but don't overdo it. The platform also reads on-screen text and captions, so it's worth using the same keywords there.

Turn on the location tag when posting, where it is available.

## Step 7: comments are a source of customers

Reply to every comment, especially in the first few hours after posting. Questions about price, opening hours or availability are really quote requests. Answer politely and invite the person to message you privately or on WhatsApp. Handle negative comments calmly and professionally: other potential customers are reading how you respond.

## Step 8: Spark Ads and local targeting

Organic content builds trust. Ads help you reach the right people in your area faster. On TikTok, a very good option for local businesses is **Spark Ads**: you promote an existing video from your account, and it appears as a normal post, with its likes and comments intact.

A few principles:

- promote videos that have already performed well organically;
- target the city or area you actually serve;
- choose a clear objective: messages, website visits or form submissions;
- test 2 or 3 different videos and keep the one that brings enquiries, not just views.

Ad budget depends on your city, industry and goal. It makes sense to start with a test budget and scale only what works.

## Step 9: measure customers, not just views

Views feel good, but they don't pay the bills. For a local business, what matters is how many people get in touch. Track:

- **phone calls**, and ask new customers how they found you;
- **WhatsApp messages**, especially if your bio link goes there;
- **direct messages** on TikTok;
- **website visits** coming from TikTok, through your site analytics;
- **bookings or orders** that mention TikTok.

A simple spreadsheet, updated weekly, will show you within a few months which type of content actually brings in customers.

## Examples by industry

### Clinic or medical practice

The doctor answers common questions in 30 seconds, in plain language. Introducing the team and the practice helps new patients feel at ease. Be mindful of professional rules on medical advertising and patient confidentiality: never film patients without their written consent.

### Restaurant or café

Dishes being prepared in the kitchen, the daily menu, guests' reactions (with their permission), a quick tour of the terrace. Always mention the city and the area. Videos with clear prices tend to drive questions and visits.

### Construction and renovation

Before-and-after series, day-by-day site progress, explanations of common mistakes and materials. Aerial footage of a finished house adds a lot of credibility.

### Beauty salon or barber

Transformations are the ideal format. Introduce each specialist, explain treatments and answer aftercare questions. Put your booking link in the bio and mention it in the video.

## Common mistakes

- Purely promotional content with nothing useful for the viewer.
- Rare, irregular posting.
- No city name in the text, so no chance in local searches.
- Comments left unanswered.
- Judging results after a week instead of a few months.
