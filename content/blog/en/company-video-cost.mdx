"How much does a company video cost?" is probably the question we hear most often. The honest answer is that it depends on what the video needs to do for your business. A 60-second clip shot in one day at your office and a brand film with actors, several locations and animation are completely different products, even if both get called a "company video".

This article breaks down where the money goes, what price ranges are realistic and how to prepare so your budget ends up where it actually matters.

## Why prices vary so much

When you ask for quotes, you will see big differences between providers. Some of that comes down to experience and equipment, but most of it comes from the fact that each quote describes a different video. Some include a script, others don't. Some deliver a single version, others adapt the footage for TikTok, Instagram and your website. Some send one person to the shoot, others bring a full crew.

So before you compare prices, compare what you actually get for each amount.

## What drives the cost

### 1. Concept and script

A good video starts on paper. This is where you define the core message, the audience, the structure, what gets filmed and what gets said. A clear script shortens the shoot and prevents reshoots. If your message is already well defined, this stage can be short. If you are starting from scratch or need a creative concept for a campaign, it takes more time and that shows up in the price.

### 2. Number of shooting days

The shooting day is one of the biggest cost factors. Most presentation videos for small and mid-sized companies can be filmed in a single day, as long as the locations are close together and the schedule is planned. Multiple sites, multiple cities or scenes that depend on a specific moment (for example, a production line that only runs at certain hours) add days and therefore cost.

### 3. Crew

A simple project can be handled by a camera operator and an assistant. A more complex one may need a director, a lead and a second camera operator, a sound recordist, someone on lighting and a makeup artist. Every extra role raises quality and speed, and the budget with it. The point is to size the crew to the project, not to how impressive it sounds.

### 4. Locations

Filming at your own premises is usually the simplest option. Renting a studio or a special venue, or travelling to another city, adds transport, accommodation and setup time. Roby Media works across Romania, and travel costs are listed openly in the quote.

### 5. Actors and on-screen talent

Many clients choose to appear on camera themselves, or have their team do it. That brings authenticity and costs nothing extra. If you need professional actors, a presenter or a voice-over artist, you have to budget for their fees and for image usage rights.

### 6. Drone footage

Aerial shots look great for factories, construction sites, hotels, farms and large properties. They also require checking the flight area, suitable weather and a dedicated pilot. Flights follow Romanian civil aviation (AACR) and EU rules and respect restricted zones, so some locations need extra preparation.

### 7. Motion graphics

Animated titles, an animated logo, infographics, explaining a process or a digital product through animation: all of this takes post-production time. A few simple on-screen texts are quick. A complex animated sequence can take as long as the shoot itself.

### 8. Music and licensing

Music has to be licensed properly, especially if the video will run in paid ads. Commercial music libraries are the usual solution. An original score or a well-known track costs considerably more, and for famous songs the license can be hard to get at all.

### 9. Social media versions

A horizontal video made for your website does not automatically work on TikTok or Reels. Vertical framing, shorter runtime, stronger opening seconds and captions make the difference. Each extra version means more editing work, but it is often the best investment of the whole project, because the same footage reaches more places.

## Where to spend and where to save

**Worth spending on:**

- a clear script, because it decides whether the video says anything useful;
- good sound, because viewers forgive an imperfect picture but not poor audio;
- vertical versions for social media, because that is where your audience sees you most.

**Ways to save:**

- shoot in a single, well-planned day;
- feature your own team instead of hiring actors;
- use library music instead of an original composition;
- keep animation simple instead of building complex sequences.

## Checklist: how to prepare

Good preparation lowers costs and improves the result. Before the first call, try to have answers to the following:

1. **Purpose.** Do you want to win new clients, recruit staff, present the company to partners or run ads?
2. **Audience.** Who needs to see the video and what convinces them?
3. **Key message.** If viewers remember only one thing, what should it be?
4. **Where it will be used.** Website, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, sales presentations, trade fairs?
5. **Length.** A main video of 60 to 120 seconds plus a few short clips covers most needs.
6. **Locations.** Which spaces you want to show and whether they are available on the day.
7. **People.** Who appears on screen and who is comfortable speaking to camera.
8. **Existing assets.** Logo, brand colours, fonts, photos, older videos.
9. **References.** Two or three videos you like, even from other industries.
10. **Rough budget and deadline.** It doesn't need to be exact, but it helps us propose the right option.

On the day, make sure the spaces are clean and tidy, your team knows about the shoot and one contact person from your side is available throughout.
